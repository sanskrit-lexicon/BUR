# PREFACE.

To oblige philologists, words of other languages analogous to the Sanskrit words have been placed at the end of many articles. But it was not wished to push these comparisons beyond evidence or the highest probability; doubtful analogies have been left to the sagacity and prudence of each.

We shall not acquaint the public with the difficulties of execution that this work has encountered, nor with the sacrifices it requires of us. We shall say only that in the printing we have been assisted beyond all hope by our skilful typographer, Mme Vve Raybois, whose Oriental Press was inaugurated in 1861 by the publication of our Sanskrit Method.

But the reader must not be unaware that the publication of this Dictionary would have been impossible without the generous support of Their Excellencies MM. Rouland and Duruy, Ministers of Public Instruction, who appreciate as they ought the high importance of Oriental studies and the future reserved for them. We should address a few words of homage to Mr James Ballantyne, for the courtesy he showed in spontaneously soliciting for us from Sir Ch. Wood, Minister of Indian Affairs, the copy of Wilson that has been the basis of this work; but death snatched him from the learned world during the course of our printing. I thank sincerely, in the name of Sanskrit studies and in my own name, M. L. Leupol for his active collaboration, M. le baron de Dumast, and most especially M. Ed. Foucaux, professor at the Collège de France, for the disinterested care with which they have helped us to correct all the proofs. We hope that this combination of conscientious efforts will have left unnoticed only a very small number of faults, and that, if any remain, they will be excused in a book which is the first Sanskrit Dictionary conceived on the plan of classical Dictionaries and the only one that has been published in France and in French to this day.

Nancy, 1 May 1865.

Em. Burnouf.

_Dr. Mārcis Gasūns_
