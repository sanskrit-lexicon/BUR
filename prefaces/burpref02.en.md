# PREFACE

This Dictionary was conceived with the aim of facilitating Oriental studies and the researches that relate to them. To attain this end, several means have been employed which the public will appreciate, and of which the principal are as follows.

The state of Sanskrit studies and the needs of philology required that the work be complete enough to take the place of the great Dictionaries of Wilson and of Saint Petersburg for those who cannot procure them. It will be seen that it does in fact contain far more material than the first edition of Wilson and, a fortiori, than the lexicons or vocabularies that are abridgments of it. One will find in it many Vedic or Buddhist terms, as well as a large number of plant or animal names and everyday expressions that are entirely wanting in the latter. Nevertheless, this Dictionary, having to remain within the ordinary conditions of classical books, does not claim to be complete: one will not find in it those fanciful compounds that abound in Indian authors and whose sense is known once one possesses their elements: the simple words being given each in its place, it will always be easy to translate one of these compounds; but we give those which have a particular and, so to speak, local meaning, which analysis alone would not discover.

In order to be useful to all philologists, even to those who, without knowing the language of India, have need of recourse to Sanskrit words, constant use has been made of the transcriptive characters adopted by the School of Nancy and which are accepted more and more
